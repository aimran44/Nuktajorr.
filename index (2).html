Why this exists

The story gets made. The writer disappears.

Pakistani drama runs on writing. The industry around it is not built to recognise that, and the people it shuts out most are the ones trying to get in.

No credit where it counts

Posters, trailers and campaigns carry the cast and the channel. The person who wrote every line of it rarely makes the frame.

Stories stretched for ad breaks

A story that should end at twenty episodes runs to forty because the slot is selling. Pacing pays for it, and so does the writing.

No way in without a contact

New writers have talent and no route. Getting read at all still depends on knowing somebody who already works there.

How it works

One place where writing and production actually meet.

Writers submit. Production houses read. Both sides get something they do not have right now.

For writers

Get read, and get named

  • Build a profile that holds your work in one place
  • Submit scripts and concepts directly to production houses
  • Keep a record of what you submitted and when you submitted it
  • Your name travels with your work, start to finish

For production houses

Find writing you cannot find anywhere else

  • Browse original scripts outside the same short list of names
  • Filter by genre, format and length before you commit to reading
  • Reach writers directly rather than through intermediaries
  • Meet audiences asking for stories you are not making yet

What viewers told us

The audience is already ahead of the industry.

We asked 23 drama viewers what they actually want. The gap between that and what gets commissioned is the whole opportunity.

83%

are tired of the mother in law versus daughter in law conflict, the single most exhausted trope in our survey

65%

want to see women in unconventional careers, the biggest content gap they named

61%

want dramas that run 20 to 30 episodes. Nobody chose 40 or more

61%

prefer a bittersweet or realistic ending over a fully happy one

Informal survey of 23 respondents run by Nuktajorr through Google Forms. Directional, not representative.

Who is behind this

Started by someone who kept noticing the same gap.

Good scripts exist. The route to the people who could produce them does not.

I am a business student, and I started Nuktajorr because I kept hearing the same thing from writers around me. They had finished work and nowhere to send it. Meanwhile production houses were looking for material and finding it through the same handful of contacts they always use.

Both sides want the same thing. Nothing connects them. That is the whole problem, and it is small enough that one person can start solving it.

I am building this alongside my degree, in public, and I would rather hear from writers and production houses now than after it is finished.

Abeer Imran Founder, Nuktajorr
